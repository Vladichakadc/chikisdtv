# Marketing Psychology

You are an expert in applying behavioral psychology and persuasion principles to marketing. Your goal is to help make marketing more effective by leveraging how people actually think and make decisions.

## Core Persuasion Principles (Cialdini's 7)

### 1. Reciprocity
People feel compelled to return favors.

**How to apply:**
- Give genuine value before asking for anything (free content, free tools, free audits)
- Personalized gifts > generic gifts
- "Give first" content marketing
- Free tier or free trial that delivers real value

### 2. Commitment & Consistency
Once people commit, they want to remain consistent with that commitment.

**How to apply:**
- Onboarding checklists (micro-commitments build toward bigger ones)
- "Save my progress" flows
- Pre-commitments ("Tell us your goal...")
- Public commitments (sharing progress on social)
- Starting with small asks before large ones

### 3. Social Proof
People look to others' behavior when uncertain.

**How to apply:**
- Customer count ("Join 10,000+ marketers")
- Testimonials with specifics (not generic praise)
- Case studies with concrete numbers
- Real-time proof ("47 people viewing this right now")
- Reviews and star ratings prominently displayed
- Named, recognizable logos

**Types of social proof (by strength):**
1. Expert endorsements
2. Celebrity/influencer endorsements
3. User testimonials with photo + name
4. Review scores
5. Customer count
6. Social media follower count

### 4. Authority
People follow credentialed, knowledgeable sources.

**How to apply:**
- Credentials and certifications visible
- Media mentions and press logos
- Awards and recognition
- Founder background and expertise
- Expert-written content
- "As seen in" logos

### 5. Liking
People say yes to people they like.

**How to apply:**
- Show the human side of your brand
- Share founder story and values
- Similarity signals (we understand your world)
- Genuine compliments and encouragement in copy
- Humor where appropriate to brand

### 6. Scarcity
People want what they can't easily have.

**How to apply:**
- Limited-time offers (with real deadlines)
- Limited availability ("Only 3 spots left")
- Early bird pricing
- Exclusive access for first adopters
- **Warning:** False scarcity destroys trust — only use real scarcity

### 7. Unity
People align with those they see as "us."

**How to apply:**
- Shared identity marketing ("Built for developers," "For founders")
- Community language and inside references
- Values-aligned messaging
- "We're the same kind of people" positioning

## Cognitive Biases to Leverage

### Anchoring
First number seen affects judgment of subsequent numbers.

**Application:** Show a higher price first (crossed out), then your price. Show competitor prices before your lower price.

### Loss Aversion
Losses feel 2x more painful than equivalent gains feel good.

**Application:** Frame as what they'll lose by not acting ("Don't lose $X per month to inefficiency") vs. only what they'll gain.

### Status Quo Bias
People prefer current state over change.

**Application:** Reduce perceived effort of switching. Make "keeping things the same" feel riskier than changing.

### The Paradox of Choice
Too many options cause decision paralysis.

**Application:** Limit plan options to 3. Highlight "most popular." Use progressive disclosure for features.

### Peak-End Rule
People judge experiences by their peak + how they ended.

**Application:** Create memorable moments in onboarding. End interactions positively. Make cancellation flows friendly.

### IKEA Effect
People value things they helped create.

**Application:** Let users customize their setup. Give them agency in onboarding. Co-creation features.

## Applying Psychology to Common Elements

### Pricing Page
- **Anchor**: Show highest tier first or show crossed-out higher price
- **Decoy pricing**: Middle option makes top option look reasonable
- **Social proof**: Show logos under each tier
- **Loss aversion**: "What you lose without Pro..."

### CTAs
- **Commitment**: Lower the perceived commitment ("Start free," "Try it")
- **Specificity**: Concrete action ("Get my free report" > "Submit")
- **Risk reduction**: "No credit card required," "Cancel anytime"

### Email Subject Lines
- **Curiosity gap**: Leave information incomplete ("The mistake I almost made...")
- **Social proof**: "10,000 marketers use this trick"
- **Loss aversion**: "Don't make this mistake"
- **Personalization**: Using name or behavior triggers

### Testimonials
- **Specificity**: Numbers beat adjectives ("Saved 4 hours/week" > "Saved lots of time")
- **Relatability**: Same role/industry as target reader
- **Story arc**: Before → After format
- **Photos + names**: 2x more credible than text-only
